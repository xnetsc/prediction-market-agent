# Time-zone information-latency strategy (experimental, unverified source claim)

This is not a high-frequency or small-order strategy. The viral March 2026 story described one
event-driven batch of six positions, allegedly deploying $12,000 after an agent had run for nine days,
with all six markets due to resolve within 90 minutes. The claimed profit and wallet history were not independently
verified; multiple copies of the story used referral-style promotion. Never treat those figures as
evidence of expected return.

Look for markets nearing resolution whose underlying event occurs outside the dominant trading
audience's active hours. The reported screen was 02:00-06:00 America/New_York and resolution within
90 minutes, but these are discovery heuristics rather than proof of edge. Search current official
local-language or local-government sources, then verify publication timestamps and whether the
information existed before the executable order-book snapshot.

Do not assume a time-zone edge exists. Require the exact contract wording, cutoff, resolution source,
oracle, dispute risk, and settlement conditions to match the reported event. Prefer two independent
public primary sources; one may suffice only when it is the contract's named authoritative source and
the result is unambiguous. Reject confidential information, alleged leaks, circular reporting,
screenshots alone, future-dated feeds, unclear translations, or sources published after the quote.

Estimate probability independently and record source timestamps. The original story claimed a
30-percentage-point edge; use at least that conservative threshold for this strategy after spread,
dynamic fees, likely slippage, and a resolution-ambiguity haircut. A cheap contract is not an edge.
Require enough two-sided depth for the requested size and avoid chasing an ask that has already moved.

Use limit orders when the evidence window permits. Size every position through the configured account
and portfolio risk engines; do not imitate the story's absolute stake. Avoid several positions whose
losses depend on the same source, announcement, jurisdiction, or failure mode unless aggregate exposure
is explicitly accounted for.

Only trade if the public fact is already observable, the market has not incorporated it, and a
conservative probability estimate still clears the threshold. Otherwise HOLD. Exit or cancel if the
source is corrected, the contract interpretation changes, executable edge disappears, or settlement
is delayed beyond the thesis.
